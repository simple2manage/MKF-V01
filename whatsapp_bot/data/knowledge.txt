Paddy cultivation in Kerala is done in three main seasons: Virippu (autumn / first crop, April-May to September-October), Mundakan (winter / second crop, September-October to December-January), and Puncha (summer / third crop, December-January to March-April). 
Rice fields in Kerala have been declining: the area under paddy dropped from about 3.22 lakh hectares in 2001-02 to 1.95 lakh hectares in 2021-22. 
Paddy production likewise decreased: from about 7.03 lakh tonnes to about 5.62 lakh tonnes over the same period in Kerala. 
Rice productivity in Kerala has fallen: average yields (in wetland paddy) were about 2,884 kg per hectare in 2021-22, which is a drop compared to earlier years. 
Paddy farming in Kuttanad (Alappuzha, Pathanamthitta, Kottayam districts) happens in land that is below sea level, with fields surrounded by backwaters; these “polder” systems are rich in biomass and yield well. 
Rice varieties are changing: in Kuttanad, the older variety Uma (MO-16) is being replaced by Pournami (MO-23), which matures more quickly and gives heavier grain yield. 
Paddy seed diversity is being preserved: in Thrissur district, an event distributed 124 varieties of paddy seeds to farmers, including 64 traditional Kerala varieties and 60 from other states. Examples include Malakkaran, Thulasi bhog, Jugal, Gandhakasala, Kunhinellu, Seethabhag, and Thavalakkannan. 
Rice (paddy) cultivation is becoming less profitable, which, along with high labour cost and less procurement by government bodies, is pushing farmers in Kerala to switch to other crops.
Paddy varieties popular in Kerala are sometimes also grown in neighbouring Tamil Nadu, because of demand. For example, farmers in Dindigul district grow varieties like Jyothi Matta, Sindu Kaveri, Akashya, which are bought by traders to be sold in Kerala. 
Rice (paddy) in South India more broadly is grown in different wetland and upland systems; Kerala has very specialized wetland-systems (like Kuttanad, Kole wetlands) whereas places in Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh etc have different irrigation, soil, and climate systems.
