Paddy requires a hot and humid climate with temperatures between 20°C and 35°C. 
Paddy grows best in clayey or alluvial soil that can retain water. The crop needs plenty of rainfall, usually between 100–200 cm, or assured irrigation for good yield. Standing water is essential during most of its growing season, and transplantation of seedlings is commonly practiced. 
Paddy is mainly grown as a kharif crop during the monsoon, but in southern states like Tamil Nadu and Andhra Pradesh, it is also cultivated as a rabi and summer crop with irrigation. 
Major rice-producing states in India include West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Bihar, Odisha, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, and Assam. Being a water-intensive crop that thrives in lowland areas, paddy is the staple food crop of India and provides the main diet for a large section of the population.
